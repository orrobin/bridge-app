title: Repairing a Conversation After a Miscommunication
author: Bridge Team (original explainer)
evidence_strength: practitioner-informed
---
Text conversations lose tone, timing, and facial expression, which makes misreadings -- a joke
landing as an insult, a short reply read as coldness -- more common than in person. What matters
most for the relationship isn't whether a misstep happens, but whether it gets acknowledged and
repaired.

An effective repair attempt tends to be short, direct, and non-defensive: naming what may have
landed wrong ("that probably came across more blunt than I meant it"), without over-explaining or
demanding immediate reassurance in return. A repair attempt that turns into a long justification,
or that pressures the other person to immediately say everything's fine, tends to prolong the
rupture rather than close it.

It's also worth noticing how a repair attempt is received: someone who brushes off a genuine,
proportionate repair attempt every time, or who uses it as an opening to escalate rather than
move on, is showing a different pattern worth paying attention to than someone who simply
accepts it and moves forward.
