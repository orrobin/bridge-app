id: positivity-ratio
title: The Balance of Positive and Negative Exchanges
author: Bridge Team (original explainer)
evidence_strength: informed-by-published-research
---
Longitudinal relationship research has repeatedly found that the ratio of positive to negative
interactions -- not the mere presence of conflict -- tends to distinguish relationships that
last from ones that don't. Occasional friction, disagreement, or an awkward exchange is normal
and not, by itself, a warning sign.

Applied loosely to an early text conversation, the useful question isn't "did anything negative
happen" but "across the whole thread, is warmth, humor, and genuine engagement clearly
outweighing friction, sarcasm, or flatness." A single terse reply in an otherwise warm,
back-and-forth conversation is very different from a thread that's consistently short, flat, or
faintly irritated.

This is a coarse heuristic for skimming an overall pattern, not a precise metric, and it says
nothing about compatibility or long-term prospects from a handful of messages -- it's meant to
help notice an overall trend across a conversation rather than fixating on any single message.
