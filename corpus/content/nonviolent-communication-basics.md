title: Observation, Feeling, Need, Request: A Simple Structure for Hard Messages
author: Bridge Team (original explainer)
evidence_strength: practitioner-informed
---
One widely used structure for saying something difficult without it landing as an accusation
breaks a message into four parts: a concrete observation (what actually happened, without
interpretation), the feeling it brought up, the underlying need behind that feeling, and a
specific request.

Compare "you never text back quickly, you clearly don't care" (interpretation presented as fact,
likely to trigger defensiveness) with "I noticed a couple of days went by without a reply, and I
started feeling unsure where things stood -- I'd like to know roughly when to expect to hear
back." The second version describes the same underlying concern without asserting a claim about
the other person's motives, which they can't actually confirm from the outside.

This structure is a tool for the user's own outgoing messages, not a device for reading someone
else's mind -- it's most useful when someone wants to raise a concern (about pace of replies,
mixed signals, or a miscommunication) without escalating it into a fight, and it applies just as
well to early dating conversations as to longer-term relationships.
