title: Active Listening in Text: Showing You Actually Read the Message
author: Bridge Team (original explainer)
evidence_strength: practitioner-informed
---
Active listening is usually described as a face-to-face skill -- eye contact, paraphrasing,
nodding -- but the underlying idea translates to text conversations too: respond to what the
other person actually said, not just to keep the exchange moving.

A few concrete markers of this in a chat thread: referencing a specific detail the other person
mentioned earlier rather than only asking generic questions; asking a follow-up that builds on
their answer instead of pivoting straight back to yourself; acknowledging an emotion they
expressed ("that sounds stressful") before responding to the practical content of the message.

The opposite pattern -- a string of messages that could have been sent regardless of what the
other person actually wrote -- often reads as distracted or self-focused, even when that isn't
the intent. In early conversations, this is one of the more controllable, low-risk things to pay
attention to: it doesn't require guessing at the other person's feelings, just noticing whether a
reply actually engages with what they said.
