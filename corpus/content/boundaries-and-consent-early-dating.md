title: Boundaries and Consent Before Meeting in Person
author: Bridge Team (original explainer)
evidence_strength: practitioner-informed
---
Early digital conversation is where pacing, comfort, and boundaries around meeting in person
first get negotiated, often implicitly. A few habits tend to make that negotiation healthier for
both people: naming a boundary or preference plainly rather than hinting at it, treating a
stated boundary as final rather than as an opening position to negotiate down, and checking in
explicitly before shifting the nature of the conversation (moving from a dating app to personal
contact details, suggesting a meetup, introducing more personal or sexual topics).

A useful marker of a healthy early dynamic is that raising a boundary doesn't produce pressure,
guilt-tripping, or repeated re-asking. If it does, that pattern is worth taking seriously as
signal about how boundaries would be handled later on, not dismissed as one bad moment.

This applies symmetrically: the same standard -- state plainly, respect what's stated, check in
before escalating -- holds for the user's own messages as much as for reading the other person's.
